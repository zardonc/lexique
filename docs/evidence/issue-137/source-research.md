## Lexical and grammatical boundaries

`surveillance` line 8 refers to `surveiller`; its fixed French definition at line 7
supplies attentive observation/control. `trahison` line 8 refers to `trahir`;
line 8 of the latter supplies faithlessness or perfidy toward someone. `auto`
line 11 refers to `automobile`; its French noun definition at line 14 supplies
the vehicle meaning. These three supporting sources have their own fixed revisions
and complete contributor records. They are not runtime aliases or extra groups.

`évanouir` line 7 identifies the exclusively pronominal link and line 16 the fainting
use. Bare destroy/dissipate at line 8 is archaic and excluded. All three explanation
fields explicitly identify `s’évanouir`; the frozen lemma/POS is preserved.
`cingler` selects the second verb’s weather meaning at line 44 and IPA at line 34,
excluding sailing, metallurgy, weapon strikes and the third verb’s chalk-line use.
`boucle` selects the first feminine noun’s belt-buckle subsense at line 16, excluding
hair curls, programming and the masculine skating noun. `bâtir` selects the second
verb’s building sense at line 42, excluding the sewing sense of the first verb.

`as` selects the card noun, with /as/ attested at line 24; the /a/ verb inflection is
excluded. `hâte` line 9 records h aspiré; no /h/ is inserted in its /ɑt/ IPA.
`mamie` selects the first noun’s affectionate/familiar/childish grandmother use,
and `planquer` retains the source’s slang label. `poésie` selects the modern genre
at line 23, preserving poetry beyond traditional verse. Pronunciation variants
for expression, territoire, renseignement, barre, navrer, auto and bâtir are selected
only where the exact IPA is attested in the matching French/POS section.
