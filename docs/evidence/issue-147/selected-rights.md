## Specific expression and factual boundaries

[37 CFR 202.1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title37-vol1/xml/CFR-2025-title37-vol1-sec202-1.xml)
distinguishes short phrases, ingredient/contents lists and common factual
information from the manner of developed writing. The
[Copyright Office Compendium](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap300/ch300-copyrightable-authorship.pdf),
sections 313.3(C), 313.4(C) and 313.4(F), makes the same distinctions: facts
are unprotected while their original expression can be protected. Each
recorded fragment or short expression is assessed individually; length or
an article’s import marker alone is not the decision.

`métal` selects the material-category, brilliance/malleability and iron/silver
facts, excluding the developed geological/chemical explanation. `tsar` keeps
title/country facts without the historical date narrative. `aiguille` keeps
rod, point, eye, thread and sewing facts. `manche` keeps garment/arm/coverage
facts. `batterie` keeps connected accumulators and direct current without the
generator-capacity-voltage sentence. `producteur` keeps the person role,
coordination/control/financing and media-product facts. `chèvre` keeps female,
male/bouc and minimal parent caprine/mammal evidence. Every other use has its
specific expression and meaning reason in the structured review.

Short expressions such as `Rôtir sur le gril`, `Introduire, mettre dans` and
`Peu souvent; peu fréquemment` are ordinary action/location/frequency glosses
with de minimis authorship. Phonetic transcriptions, POS/gender/inflection
labels and the three-word `Semblable au` construction are linguistic facts
or short conventional forms. No quotation body or sound recording is selected.
French explanations and new EN/ZH wording convey the accepted lexical facts.
