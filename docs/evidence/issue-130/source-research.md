## Spelling, construction and meaning boundaries

`führer/NOM` uses the exact lowercase French common noun: a dictator or someone
acting like one, explicitly pejorative. Its French article and `/fy.ʁœʁ/` supply
the source; German pronunciation, general German “leader” and uppercase historical
`Führer` are excluded. This is proposed ordinary French figurative use, with its
register retained, not an approved specialist exception.

`grec/ADJ` relates to Greece, its people or language; `grec/NOM` names the ancient
or modern language. The uppercase person demonym is not substituted. The two
`lesquelles` groups use separate feminine plural interrogative and relative
frames from the exact form, with definitions and agreement tables from `lequel`.
The relative use's prepositional constructions remain separately evidenced.

`la plupart des/ADJ:ind` is the complete quantifier expression before a plural
noun. Lexique383.tsv original line 75479 attests that spelling and exact subclass.
Its `phon=laplypaRdE` and `syll=la-ply-paR-dE` yield `/la.ply.paʁ.dɛ/` using the
manual page 12 Table 2 (R=/ʁ/, E=/ɛ/); page 13 Table 3 defines ADJ:ind. This is
Lexique's own phonemic code, not an X-SAMPA claim. [Exact dataset evidence](lexique-phrase-source.json)
retains the original row, dataset provenance and fixed manual commit.
Wiktionnaire's `plupart` definition supplies majority meaning, mandatory `la`
and agreement with the following noun; the component is labelled a noun there.
That distinction is explicit, and the frozen expression remains ADJ:ind.
The absent standalone article and `la plupart` redirect capture are retained in
the ignored evidence. No alias or replacement of the frozen headword is proposed.

`priver` retains its person object and `de` complement; `correspondre` selects
message exchange with `avec`, not matching things. `geler`, `peser`, `sucer`,
`renverser` and `imposer` select their stated transitive uses. `bagnole` retains
familiar French/Belgian/Swiss usage, `guerrier` retains the source's taking pleasure
in war, and `bonbon` retains the broader children's meaning. The source variants
for `canard`, `guerrier` and `médecine` remain visible in the raw spans; one variant
is selected. `estomac` retains the attested silent final c. Bare `dé` means the
game die; thimble and letter-name senses are excluded. `chic`, `grec`, `pratique`
and `compliqué` use their adjective frames, never neighbouring noun or verb frames.
