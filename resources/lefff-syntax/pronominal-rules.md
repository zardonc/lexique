# Pronominal construction admission

Modification, 2026-11-06: Lexique projects separate pronominal identities and complete expressions from the pinned Lefff syntax/morphology and Grammalecte data. Six MIT converter modules accompany the generated database. Original data, copyright notices and licenses remain unchanged; this policy and independently implemented rules do not incorporate upstream executable code.

Admission requires Grammalecte's explicit optional/obligatory/reciprocal/pronominal-with-en flag, or an explicit Lefff `@pron`, `seréfl` or `seréc` frame. Special-case flags alone do not admit a use. A `%se_moyen` passive construction alone does not license an ordinary reflexive paradigm. Source entry/line references and type codes remain attached. Positive pronominal frames in the latest source take precedence for person restrictions. Pronominal-only identities retain their ordinary-use exclusion. No frequency cap applies; every original lemma receives an admission or exclusion report.

Pronouns agree with the verbal person. Lexical aspirated-h and y elision use the existing pinned RosaeNLG data. Affirmative imperative reflexives follow the verb with a hyphen; the admitted `en` construction uses `-t’en / -nous-en / -vous-en`. Finite expressions retain complete subject and subjunctive alternatives. Reciprocal-only entries exclude first/second singular and use collective `on` for third singular. Explicit impersonal frames restrict the subject to `il`; finite morphology patterns preserve restricted persons. See [OQLF pronominal use](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22939/la-grammaire/le-verbe/verbes-pronominaux/emploi-de-la-forme-pronominale) and [imperative pronouns](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24206/la-grammaire/les-pronoms/pronoms-personnels/pronoms-personnels-employes-avec-un-verbe-a-limperatif).

All pronominal compounds use être. Explicit participle gender/number dimensions supply variants; no suffix is invented. Essentially/obligatorily pronominal frames without a direct object follow subject agreement. Direct-object or unresolved optional frames retain the reference participle and separately addressable, labelled contextual gender/number alternatives; no absent object is inferred. A reflexive indirect object without a competing direct reflexive or other direct-object frame retains an invariable reference. Bare participles can belong to both ordinary and pronominal tables. Missing source dimensions remain missing, and grammatical restrictions are reported separately. [OQLF essential agreement](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22940/la-grammaire/le-verbe/accord-du-participe-passe/avec-lauxiliaire-etre/accord-du-participe-passe-des-verbes-essentiellement-pronominaux), [context-dependent agreement](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22942/la-grammaire/le-verbe/accord-du-participe-passe/avec-lauxiliaire-etre/accord-du-participe-passe-des-verbes-occasionnellement-pronominaux), and [Académie agreement](https://www.dictionnaire-academie.fr/article/QDL069) establish the independent expectations.

Verified exceptions: `se plaire`, `se complaire`, `se déplaire` and `se rire` retain invariable participles, as described by [Usito](https://usito.usherbrooke.ca/articles/aides_%C3%A0_la_r%C3%A9daction/LAccordDuParticipePass%C3%A9). `s’arroger` has a direct-object frame and therefore retains context-dependent agreement despite its obligatory-pronominal flag. The `en` construction follows subject agreement for aller/venir/revenir and uses the invariant reference for vouloir/revouloir/falloir. `en découdre` is an ordinary locution, so its ambiguous `en` flag does not produce a fabricated `s’en découdre` identity; the original ordinary forms remain available.

Subject-agreeing compounds admit plural participles with collective `on`, while retaining a third-singular finite verb; they do not license plural participles with `il/elle`. See [OQLF agreement with on](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21708/la-grammaire/ladjectif/accord-de-ladjectif/accord-de-ladjectif-qui-qualifie-un-pronom-personnel/accord-de-ladjectif-avec-le-pronom-on). Impersonal-only uses retain the invariant reference. Where explicitly supported, bare-reflexive and `en` constructions receive distinct identities: `se vouloir` and `s’en vouloir` coexist. Lefff's explicit pronominal frame admits `se découdre`, while the non-reflexive locution `en découdre` does not admit `s’en découdre`.

The resource establishes source-backed reference constructions and labelled context alternatives, not arbitrary sentence analysis or universal certification of every lexical sense. English meanings use only explicit pronominal source headwords for the new identities, including `s’en aller`; ordinary meanings and source relationships remain separate. New aliases never merge learning records or perform implicit saves.
